Timeline - Lamoni Rose Hill Cemetery

The timeline begins with key events (in italic) that precede the first burial at the cemetery in 1881 and when it was formally established as the city cemetery with the name Rose Hill Cemetery in 1885.

1870 - Gathering a Community

The Order of Enoch was organized as a group of shareholders, lead by the Hon. Elijah Banta and leaders like David Dancer to quietly purchase land that would be distributed to establish a community of gathering for the RLDS church, a.k.a. the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints (now called the Community of Christ). The RLDS church was the largest LDS group who did not follow Brigham Young to Utah after the murder of its founder Joseph Smith Jr. There was already a post office here named Sedgwick, but the new town was named Lamoni. Banta and many others arrived from Sandwich, Illinois, or smaller gatherings in western Iowa, which places appear in the obituaries for early settlers buried at Rose Hill. the birthplace of early burials at Rose Hill came from there. Old obituaries and news articles often referred to the LDS church rather than the RLDS church, bolstered by early members seeing their group as a continuation of the original church, especially since the original founder's son, Joseph Smith III, was leading it. But RLDS became more insistently used to distinguish it from the Utah church.

1879 - Lamoni is Established

The railroad was built and the town of Lamoni was laid out.

Feb 1880 - 1st Lamoni funeral

The first funeral in the newly laid out town of Lamoni was for Nehemiah D. Smith. Rose Hill did not yet exist so he was likely buried 2 miles south in Sweet Home Cemetery, but was relocated to Rose Hill Cemetery years later.

Nov 1881 - 1st RoseHill Burial

First burial at what will become Rose Hill Cemetery

Oct 1885 - Rose Hill Founded

First portion of land purchased from David and Rosalia Dancer to establish a cemetery entitled Rose Hill Cemetery whose trustees are Elijah Banta (pres), Rosalia Dancer (VP), and Seth Bass (Secr).

1922 - Land Deed Correction

From the beginning it was assumed that the cemetery extended 40 rods (660') north of Main Street for the adjacent pieces of land aquired in 1885 and 1998. But the 1885 piece stopped short at 588' and even the land owners thought that since Rosalia Dancer was a board trustee. The Dancer heirs fixed this by re-deeding the 1885 piece to be 20x40 rods. Besides correcting the north border, it pushed the border another 30' east which added another row of burial lots and a north-south access road.

1922 - Old Cemetery Expands

Land was purchased from the RLDS church which almost doubled the size of the cemetery, becoming we now call the Old (or West) Cemetery. It encompasses all cemetery property from the shed to the westmost edge and south to Main Street.
